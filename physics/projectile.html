1 · Split the launch in two

ux = u cos θ = 25 × cos 40° = m s⁻¹
uy = u sin θ = × sin ° = m s⁻¹
ax = 0    ay = −9.81 m s⁻²

This single step is the whole topic. Gravity pulls straight down, so it can change vy and nothing else — which means the horizontal and vertical motions never talk to each other. You then solve two easy one-dimensional problems instead of one hard two-dimensional one, and the only thing the two share is the clock.

2 · The vertical problem

at the top vy = 0, so t = uy/g = s
max height = h + uy²/2g = m
landing: 0 = h + uyt − ½gt² → T = s

The apex is defined by vy = 0, not by v = 0 — the projectile is still moving at full horizontal speed up there. Launch from ground level and the flight is symmetrical, so T is exactly twice the time to the apex; launch from a height and that symmetry breaks, which is why the landing needs the quadratic.

3 · The horizontal problem

R = ux × T = × = m
from level ground: R = u² sin 2θ / g = m

Horizontally there is no acceleration at all, so distance is just speed × time — and the time comes entirely from the vertical problem. The sin 2θ form explains two famous facts at once: the range peaks at 45° because sin 90° = 1, and complementary angles like 30° and 60° give identical ranges because sin 60° = sin 120°.

Watch vx in the graph: a flat horizontal line until you switch drag on. The energy row is ½v² + gy, the kinetic and potential energy of each kilogram — without drag it is a constant, with drag it bleeds away into the air. That is the cleanest way to see what drag actually does.

Try this: set the height to 0 and sweep the angle — the range peaks at exactly 45°, and 30° gives the same range as 60°. Turn on the ½v² + gy trace and watch it sit perfectly flat: energy is conserved, it is only being traded between the kinetic and potential forms. Now switch on air resistance and that line sags, the peak drops, the descent gets steeper than the climb, and the best angle falls below 45°. The green arrows are the velocity components: vx never changes without drag, while vy passes through zero at the top.
