1 · Resolving the weight

W = mg = 5.0 × 9.81 = 49.1 N
N = mg cos θ = × cos 25° = N
mg sin θ = W × sin 25° = N

A surface can only push at right angles to itself, so the weight has to be split into a part pressing into the slope — exactly balanced by N, since there is no acceleration in that direction — and a part running along the slope, which is the only piece trying to move the block. Notice N is not mg once the slope is tilted.

2 · Does it move at all?

fmax = μsN = 0.60 × = N
driving force = mg sin θ − F = − 0 = N
—
limiting angle = tan⁻¹ μs = °

Static friction is not a fixed number — it grows to match whatever is pulling, up to a ceiling of μsN. Set mg sin θ = μsN and the mass cancels on both sides, leaving tan θ = μs. That is why a heavier block does not grip the slope any better.

3 · Once it is sliding

fk = μdN = 0.40 × = N
ma = mg sin θ − F − fk = N
a = ÷ = m s⁻²

Kinetic friction is locked at μdN and always points against the motion, so it no longer adjusts itself. Because μd < μs for almost every pair of surfaces, the friction force drops the instant the block breaks away — which is why things tend to lurch into motion rather than easing into it.

4 · Right now

t = 0 s   x = 0 m   v = 0 m s⁻¹
check with v² = u² + 2ax  →  v = 0 m s⁻¹
KE = ½mv² = 0 J
work against friction = f x = 0 J

While the block is sliding, a is constant, so the SUVAT equations apply and the two values of v above must agree. The energy line is the other way of seeing the same motion: gravity does mgx sin θ of work on the block, friction takes f x of it away as heat, and whatever is left over appears as kinetic energy.

Try this: leave the push at zero and raise the angle one degree at a time. The block stays put while mg sin θ is below μsN, then breaks away the moment you pass tan⁻¹μs — and changing the mass does not move that threshold at all, because m cancels. Watch the orange arrow: below the limit it grows to match the pull exactly, then drops to the fixed value μdN once sliding starts. Now add a push: find the force that just holds it still, then one that drives it back up — friction flips direction to oppose the new motion, and the acceleration graph jumps.
