1 · Moment of inertia

I = Ibody + 2mr²
= + 2 × × ()² = kg m²

Moment of inertia is rotation's version of mass: how hard it is to change the spin. But unlike mass it is not a fixed property of the object — it depends on where the mass sits relative to the axis, and on r², so moving the hands halfway in cuts their contribution to a quarter.

2 · Angular momentum is conserved

L = Iω, and with no external torque L cannot change
I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂
× = × =
ω has grown by a factor

The skater's own muscles are internal forces — they come in equal and opposite pairs and cannot change the total. Only an outside torque could, and the ice provides almost none. So whatever I does, ω must do the exact opposite to keep the product fixed.

3 · But energy is not

KE = ½Iω² = L²/2I
arms out: J → now J
check via L²/2I = J
work done by the skater = J

Writing it as L²/2I is the key step: with L pinned, kinetic energy is inversely proportional to I, so halving I doubles the energy. That energy is not free — it is work the arms do pulling the masses inwards against the force needed to hold them in a circle. Let go and the arms fly out, handing the energy straight back.

4 · Right now

ω = rad s⁻¹ → period = s
speed of the hands v = ωr = m s⁻¹

Note that while ω rises as the arms come in, v = ωr can still fall, because r is shrinking too. The hands turn faster but travel a much smaller circle. Angular and linear speed are genuinely different quantities, and this is the cleanest place to see them come apart.

Try this: hit pull in / extend and watch the four bars. The blue one — angular momentum — never moves, because nothing exerts an external torque on the skater. I falls as r², so ω rises by exactly the factor I falls by. The graph says the same thing across every radius at once: a flat blue line, with the orange and red curves crossing it in opposite directions. Kinetic energy is not conserved — ½Iω² = L²/2I, so halving I doubles the energy, and that extra energy is real work done by the arms. Set the hand masses to zero and nothing changes with r: with no mass out at radius r there is nothing for the r² to act on.
