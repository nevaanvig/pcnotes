1 · The three links

gradient of s–t  =  v
gradient of v–t  =  a
area under v–t  =  displacement

These are not three separate rules, they are one idea read in both directions. Differentiating takes you down the stack (position → velocity → acceleration); integrating takes you back up, which is why area appears. Everything else in kinematics is bookkeeping on top of this.

2 · SUVAT, stage by stage

v = u + at    s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as    s = ½(u+v)t

These only work while a is constant, which is why the motion is built in stages — each stage has its own constant a, and the velocity at the end of one becomes the u of the next. The table above shows that handover explicitly.

3 · Displacement vs distance

displacement = m  (area with sign)
distance = m  (area ignoring sign)
these are —
average velocity = s/t = m s⁻¹
average speed = d/t = m s⁻¹

Displacement is a vector and distance is a scalar, and the v–t graph is where you can actually see the difference: area below the axis counts as negative for one and positive for the other. Make stage 3 long enough for the velocity to go negative and the two numbers separate immediately.

4 · Right now

t = 0 s
s = 0 m   distance so far = 0 m
v = 0 m s⁻¹   a = 0 m s⁻²

Follow the dashed cursor across all four graphs at once. Where the v–t graph crosses zero the s–t curve has a horizontal tangent — that is the turning point, and it is the moment the displacement and distance lines in the lower graph start to diverge.

Try this: leave stage 1 at +2 and make stage 3 exactly −2 for the same duration — the v–t graph becomes a symmetrical triangle and the object ends at rest. Now make stage 3 run longer: the velocity goes negative, the shaded area turns from blue to amber and starts subtracting from the displacement, and the s–t curve turns over and comes back down. In the lower graph the displacement and distance lines split apart at exactly that moment. Watch the dashed tangent: wherever it is horizontal, the middle graph crosses zero. And notice the s–t graph is only ever straight while a = 0 — any curvature at all means an acceleration.
