1 · The sphere itself

V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)π(0.050)³ = m³
m = ρV = 600 × = kg
W = mg = N

Everything starts from the volume, because both the weight and the upthrust are proportional to it. That is the hidden reason size never decides whether something floats: scale the sphere up and V multiplies both sides of the comparison equally.

2 · Archimedes' principle

Fb = ρf Vsub g
Vsub = % of V = m³
Fb = 1000 × × 9.81 = N
ρ/ρf =   —

The upthrust is the weight of the fluid pushed out of the way — nothing more. Floating means that weight of displaced fluid has grown to equal the object's own weight, and since both are ρVg, the fraction left under the surface is exactly ρ/ρf. Ice sits 92% submerged for precisely this reason.

3 · Fluid resistance

viscous: 6πηrv = 6π × × × 0 = 0 N
form: ½CdρfAv² = 0 N
total drag = 0 N
Re = ρfvd/η = 0

Stokes' law (the 6πηrv term) is the one the syllabus names, but it is only honest for very slow, small, sticky flow — Reynolds numbers below about 1. A 5 cm ball dropped in water is nowhere near that, so the ½CdρAv² term takes over and is what actually sets the speed. Switch it off in the controls and watch the predicted terminal speed become absurd.

4 · Terminal speed, and right now

at terminal speed: W − Fb = Fdrag
W − Fb = N  →  vt = m s⁻¹
net force now = N
a = net ÷ m = ÷ = m s⁻²

Terminal speed is not a property of the object — it is just the speed at which the drag has grown enough to cancel whatever is left of the weight after upthrust. The acceleration line shows the approach: it starts at its largest value and decays towards zero, which is why the velocity graph flattens into a horizontal line rather than ever truly arriving.

Try this: the float-or-sink verdict depends only on the density ratio and never on size — a steel marble and a steel cannonball both sink. Set the object denser than the fluid and press Drop in from above: it falls, hits the surface, and the velocity graph bends over as drag catches up with the weight. Then set it lighter and Release at the bottom — it rises, breaches, and bobs, each bounce smaller than the last because drag is removing energy every time. Crank the viscosity to honey levels and the bobbing disappears entirely: the motion becomes overdamped, and the sphere simply creeps to its resting depth.
