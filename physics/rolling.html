1 · Energy has two places to go

mgh = ½mv² + ½Iω²
rolling without slipping: ω = v/R
and I = kMR², so ½Iω² = ½kmv²
mgh = ½mv²(1 + k)

A rolling object is doing two things at once — moving and spinning — and the drop in height has to pay for both. The shape factor k = I/MR² is the whole story: it says how much rotational energy the object demands for every unit of forward motion.

2 · Speed at the bottom

v = √(2gh / (1 + k))
for the solid sphere: k =
h = L sin θ = × sin ° = m
v = m s⁻¹

Both m and R cancel completely — a marble and a bowling ball of the same shape arrive at exactly the same speed. Only k matters, and k depends purely on how the mass is arranged relative to the axis, not on how much of it there is.

3 · Acceleration and time

a = g sin θ / (1 + k) = / = m s⁻²
a is constant, so L = ½at² → t = s

Because a is constant the whole way down, every SUVAT equation applies. Compare a with g sin θ, the frictionless slide: the 1 + k in the denominator is the toll that spinning charges. A hoop pays double.

4 · Where the energy ends up

rotational share = k/(1+k) = %
translational share = 1/(1+k) = %
check: gh = = ½v²(1+k) = J kg⁻¹

A solid sphere diverts only 29% of its energy into spin, a disc a third, a hoop a full half — which is exactly the finishing order, every time. The sliding block diverts none at all, so it always wins. Friction is what makes the rolling objects spin, but it does no work here: the contact point is instantaneously stationary, so no energy is lost to it.

Try this: race them all. The order never changes, because a = g sin θ / (1 + I/MR²) contains no mass and no radius — a marble beats a cannonball-sized hoop every time. The speed graph shows why: four straight lines from the origin with different gradients, and those gradients are the accelerations. The frictionless sliding block always wins outright, since none of its energy is diverted into spinning.
