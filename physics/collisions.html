1 · Momentum is always conserved

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
× + × = kg m s⁻¹
after: kg m s⁻¹ ✓

During the collision the two carts push on each other with equal and opposite forces for exactly the same length of time, so the impulses they receive are equal and opposite and the total momentum cannot change. Nothing about the squashiness of the carts enters into it, which is why this one holds for every collision.

2 · Restitution sorts the rest

One equation is never enough for two unknowns, so you need a second fact about how bouncy the collision is. That is what e measures: the fraction of the approach speed that survives as separation speed. e = 1 is a perfect bounce, e = 0 means they stay stuck together.

3 · Solving the pair

v₁ = [(m₁ − em₂)u₁ + (1+e)m₂u₂] / (m₁+m₂) = m s⁻¹
v₂ = [(m₂ − em₁)u₂ + (1+e)m₁u₁] / (m₁+m₂) = m s⁻¹

These look fearsome but they are just the two equations above solved simultaneously. Two special cases are worth memorising: equal masses with e = 1 simply swap velocities, and a light object bouncing off a very heavy one comes back at almost its original speed.

The lost energy went into heat, sound and permanent dents — it did not vanish, it just left the mechanical account. The last line is the elegant way to see a collision: watch from a frame moving with the centre of mass (the purple marker, which glides on at constant speed through everything) and the two velocities simply reverse and shrink by a factor e.

Try this: turn on the momentum and energy traces and press Play. Momentum is a flat line straight through the collision; kinetic energy drops off a cliff at the moment of impact unless e = 1. Now slide e from 1 down to 0 and watch that cliff grow — at e = 0 the carts move off locked together with the largest possible loss. Two cases worth seeing: equal masses with e = 1 and u₂ = 0, where the carts simply swap velocities; and the purple centre-of-mass marker, which slides on at a perfectly constant speed no matter how violent the collision is.
