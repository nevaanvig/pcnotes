1 · What a moment is

moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot
measured in N m — and it is not a joule

A force's turning effect depends on where it acts, not just how big it is. Perpendicular matters: only the part of the distance at right angles to the force counts, which is why a push aimed straight at the hinge of a door does nothing at all however hard you shove.

2 · Every term, right now

—
pivot at x = 2.00 m

Each load contributes force × its own distance from the pivot, and which side of the pivot it sits on decides whether that contribution turns the beam clockwise or anticlockwise. A load sitting exactly on the pivot contributes nothing, because its distance is zero.

3 · The two conditions for equilibrium

Στ: anticlockwise = clockwise N m
difference = N m
ΣF: N = total weight = N

Balancing the moments alone is not enough — that only stops it rotating. The forces must cancel too, or the whole beam would accelerate off the pivot. Both conditions have to hold at once, and the second one is what tells you the force on the pivot.

4 · Solving for the unknown

moments of everything except B, about the pivot: N m
B must cancel that: mBg(xB − xp) = −
—
B is currently at m, mass kg

This is the standard exam move: take moments about a point, write Σ anticlockwise = Σ clockwise, and solve for whatever is missing. Choosing the pivot as your point is deliberate — the pivot's own reaction force has zero distance from it, so it drops out of the equation entirely and you never need to know it.

Try this: put 4 kg at 0.5 m and the pivot at 2.0 m, then read off where card 4 says load B has to sit — then slide B there and watch the difference hit zero. It lands at 3.0 m, because 39.2 × 1.5 = 19.6 × 3.0. Only the ratio of the distances matters, so doubling a mass halves its distance. The sweep graph shows the same thing as a picture: the green line crosses zero exactly once, and that crossing is the balance point. Set the beam's own mass above zero and it acts as one extra load at the centre, 2.0 m — harmless while the pivot is centred, but the moment you move the pivot it starts pulling its own weight.
